Assumptions

  • Geometry: the forearm is one straight prismatic beam of length d (radius and ulna lumped), rectangular section bw × h. Sample 37.5 × 25 mm: replace with a realistic section.
  • Supports: pin at the elbow (x = 0) and load W at the hand (x = d). Upper arm fixed and vertical.
  • Muscle force: a uniform line load over the patch [x₁, x₂] on the muscle-side surface, centred on the insertion (x = a). Its direction is fixed along the line from the patch to the shoulder.
  • Eccentric axial part: the axial component acts at y = h/2, not on the neutral axis, so it adds a distributed couple (checkbox above).
  • Statics: the muscle force comes from the moment balance about the elbow, so the beam is in equilibrium.
  • Material and kinematics: linear elastic isotropic bone (E ≈ 17 GPa, ν ≈ 0.30), small deflection, Euler–Bernoulli theory.
  • Stress state: plane problem, σyy ≈ σzz ≈ 0 away from the loads.
  • Neglected: forearm weight, muscle wrapping, tendon compliance, dynamics.

Equations used

V(x) = Ry + qt·⟨x−x₁⟩ (to x₂), M(x) = Ryx + ∫qt(x−s)ds + (h/2)·Fa share, N(x) = −(Rx + qa⟨x−x₁⟩)
σxx = N/A − M y / I, τxy = −V Q(y) / (I bw)
Sign convention: x along the forearm, y toward the muscle side; N tension positive; V = sum of transverse forces left of the cut (up positive); M sagging positive.
