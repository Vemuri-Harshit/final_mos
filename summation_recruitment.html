Temporal Summation & Motor Unit Recruitment

A single twitch is weak and brief, so how does the body pull harder on purpose? On the left, fire one motor unit faster and watch its twitches add up into a tetanus; on the right, recruit more motor units, smallest first, and turn the active share of the muscle into force with a stress–strain model.

Click a pane to open it full size. The takeaway: the nervous system grades force in two ways, by firing each motor unit faster (temporal summation, up to a fused tetanus) and by recruiting more motor units (size principle). Together they set how much of the muscle's cross-section is active, and so the force the elbow model needs.
